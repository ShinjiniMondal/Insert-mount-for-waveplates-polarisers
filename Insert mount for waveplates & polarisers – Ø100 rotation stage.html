Insert mount for waveplates and polarisers

A drop-in mount for the central bore of the Ø100 motorised rotation stage (42BYGH613 drive). A short spigot centres the optic on the rotation axis, four M4 screws in the table's own threaded holes hold it in any orientation, and the optic sits just above the table face with open clear aperture front and back.

    Assembly and alignment

      Why it is built this way

      Centred by a short pilot, seated by a face

      The spigot is a sliding fit (0.02–0.05 mm clearance) in the stage bore, so the optic's centre sits on the rotation axis and the beam does not walk as the table turns. The flange face lying on the table face sets the tilt. It is kept deliberately short (12 of the 31 mm available): a long tight spigot plus a face contact would over-constrain the part and make it rock or jam. The relief groove under the flange lets the face sit fully flat even if the lathe leaves a small corner radius.

      Held by what the stage already has

      The rotating table carries four M4 threads on a 34 × 34 mm square (4-M4-7H in the drawing), which are empty in your photo. The mount screws into those, so it cannot slide out of the unthreaded bore whether the stage lies flat or stands on its side. Nothing on the stage is drilled or modified. The four larger cap screws visible next to those holes most likely hold the table to its worm wheel – leave them in place; the flange underside has a 0.8 mm relief beyond Ø56 so it clears their heads.

      Optic just above the table, open on both sides

      The optic sits in a pocket in the flange, so its back face is only a couple of millimetres above the table face instead of deep in the 31 mm bore. Behind it, the hollow spigot and the stage bore are open all the way to the back, so the optic is visible and usable from the front and from the back.

      Gentle, repeatable clamping

      Waveplates and polarisers are sensitive to mechanical stress: squeezing the glass or crystal induces birefringence, which changes the retardance of a waveplate and spoils the extinction ratio of a polariser. The optic is therefore never clamped metal-on-glass. The retaining ring presses an FKM O-ring onto the optic edge, and the ring bottoms out metal-to-metal on the flange face, so the O-ring is always compressed by the same 0.2 mm no matter how hard the screws are turned. The O-ring presses directly above the seat ledge, so support and load act on the same radius and the optic does not bend.

      Built to last

      EN AW-6082 T6 (or 6061-T6) aluminium is stiff, machines well, and expands with temperature at the same rate as the aluminium stage, so the fit does not change between a cold lab and a warm one. Black anodising makes the surface hard and corrosion-resistant and cuts stray reflections near the beam. Stainless A2 screws do not rust, and the M4 threads get at least 1.5 × d of engagement. PLA test prints are fine for checking the fit but should not stay in service: PLA creeps under the constant O-ring load and softens around 55–60 °C.

      Balanced and light

      The mount is rotationally symmetric and weighs a few tens of grams, far below the load rating of a stage this size, so it does not unbalance the worm drive or cause backlash-related errors.
